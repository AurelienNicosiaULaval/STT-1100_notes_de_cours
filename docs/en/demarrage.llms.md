## Three pieces, three functions

Adventures, challenges and exercises do not serve the same purpose. Distinguishing them prevents you from looking for the wrong answer in the wrong place.

Adventure

### Learn through a situation

You are guided through a narrative context: a role, someone to help, data to understand and choices to justify.

Challenge

### Show your autonomy

The challenge remains connected to the adventure, but asks for a shorter, more focused and assessed personal production.

Exercises

### Stabilize habits

The exercises stand apart. They use other contexts to practise without depending on the challenge narrative.

## Progression in four phases

Difficulty increases by layers. Technical skills come back, but the level of autonomy and communication responsibility increases.

Modules 1-3

### Enter the craft

Produce reproducible reports, understand data structure and turn analysis into a message.

Modules 4-6

### Make work reliable

Clean, document, join tables and collaborate without losing reproducibility.

Modules 7-8

### Act responsibly

Visualize without misleading, protect data and automate information collection carefully.

Modules 9-10

### Communicate uncertainty

Make a first prediction, discuss bias and deliver an analysis others can consult.

## Data Science Immersion

The modules place you in the roles that structure a data science project. The goal is to learn technical gestures while understanding who produces, protects, analyzes and communicates data.

![Full data science project cycle: import, tidy, transform, visualize, model and communicate.](autre_materiel/Présentation%20des%20différents%20rôles/whole-game.png)

### Data Engineer

Make reliable, accessible and well-structured data available.

### Data Owner

Guarantee governance, quality, access and confidentiality.

### Statistician

Model phenomena, quantify uncertainty and formulate careful conclusions.

### Data Scientist

Turn data into analyses, products, models or recommendations.

### Data Analyst

Produce indicators, visualizations and analyses useful for decision-making.

### Data Journalist

Tell verifiable and visual stories from documented data.

### Freelancer

Intervene on part of the cycle with targeted and adaptable expertise.

## Minimal Routine

Each work session should follow a simple routine. It reduces errors and keeps the project reproducible.

1.  Open the right project in RStudio.
2.  Read the learning-plan card linked to the task of the day.
3.  Run code in small sections and check the outputs.
4.  Render the Quarto document to catch errors.
5.  Keep only useful files, then commit if the module requires it.

### When you are stuck

Reproduce the error, read the full message, then isolate the smallest possible example.

### With the course AI

Ask for an explanation, a check or a debugging lead. Always verify the answer before using it.

### Before submitting

Render the file, reread the conclusions and check that the deliverable answers the challenge.
